Dynamic Stochastic General Equilibrium in Dual-Track Transition Economies: Price Liberalization and Factor Misallocation

Dr. Rustam Karimov 1*, Prof. Elena S. Voronova 2, Alisher K. Mukhamedov 1

1 Center for Economic Research and Structural Reforms, Tashkent; 2 Vienna Institute for International Economic Studies (WIIW)

Abstract: This paper develops a two-sector dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) model calibrated to examine transitional price liberalization in the presence of sticky administrative tariffs and industrial capital subsidies. Applying Bayesian estimation to quarterly macro data (2010–2025), we establish that partial factor adjustments generate an initial welfare deadweight loss offset by medium-term Total Factor Productivity (TFP) gains of 4.2% within five fiscal cycles.

Non-Parametric Efficiency Frontiers in Public Healthcare Provision: Generalized Directional Distance Function Estimation

Jamshid N. Tursunov 1, Prof. Claudia H. Meier 2

1 Westminster International University in Tashkent; 2 University of Zurich

Abstract: Evaluating sub-national public healthcare expenditure efficiency requires addressing endogenous input adjustments and environmental heterogeneity. Using bootstrap Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) with order-m robust estimators across 14 administrative oblasts, we demonstrate that structural resource consolidation enhances output yields by 14.8% without increased budget appropriations.

Fiscal Decentralization, Sovereign Debt Sustainability, and Intergovernmental Transfer Mechanics in Central Asia

Dilnoza M. Rakhimova 1, Prof. Boris G. Danilov 2

1 Tashkent State University of Economics; 2 Graduate School of Public Policy, Nazarbayev University

Abstract: This research analyzes structural reforms in fiscal federalism and equalization grants in post-Soviet Central Asian republics. Employing a panel vector autoregression (PVAR) across fifteen years of municipal finance registries, the study reveals that unconditional formulaic grants curb subnational deficit monetization and lower sovereign borrowing spreads by an average of 38 basis points.

The Middle Corridor and Eurasian Trade Reconfiguration: A Structural Gravity Modeling Approach with Poisson Pseudo-Maximum Likelihood

Sardor B. Kasymov 1*, Prof. Hans-Jürgen Becker 2

1 Institute for Fiscal Studies and Logistics Economics; 2 University of Hamburg

Abstract: Geopolitical shifts have renewed focus on multimodal transit routes crossing the Caspian basin. Estimating structural gravity models via Poisson Pseudo-Maximum Likelihood (PPML) across 62 bilateral partner pairs between 2018 and 2025, we quantify tariff and non-tariff friction reductions, projecting an annualized intra-regional export expansion of $3.6 billion upon full digital customs integration.
